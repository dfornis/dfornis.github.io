Nordic datacenter tracker

This page lists publicly reported plans for new datacenters in the Nordic countries covered by the project register and estimates how much electricity they would use. The data come from company announcements, news reports, planning and permit documents, and grid operators. I am still developing the method. Comments, corrections and missing projects are welcome at fornborg@kth.se.

Planned datacenter sites

Announced capacity at new datacenter sites, as planned when each site is finished. Confirmed, Dated plans and All announced set how far a plan must have come to be included.

Confirmed has capacity with an investment decision or a construction start. Dated plans adds plans with a date but no decision. All announced adds everything else. Some values are ranges, as explained under Ranges. Circles show sites and the sand shading shows the total for each bidding zone. The circles, shading and bars use the upper value of the quantified capacity. Capacity is the estimated power of the whole site, including cooling and other auxiliary power needs. Electricity use is the yearly use once all capacity is built and in full use. Click a country to enlarge it, and a circle to see its projects. Full method below.

How the numbers are calculated

Parameters and assumptions

Capacity is expressed as site power: the electrical capacity of the whole datacenter, including cooling. The tracker subtracts capacity already operating at the end of 2025 and counts overlapping figures once.

Ranges

The tracker shows a range when project sizes, dates or operating conditions are uncertain.

Source of uncertainty How it affects the estimate
Reported capacity If a source reports 250–500 MW, the tracker uses 250 MW for the lower estimate and 500 MW for the upper estimate.
Existing capacity Some of the announced total may already be operating. If that amount is unknown, the estimate of new capacity runs from zero to the reported total.
Load factor Large campuses with several physical tenants, and campuses whose size or use is unclear, use a load factor of 0.57–0.75. This widens the electricity estimate without changing the capacity estimate.
Peak demand When a source reports peak demand, installed capacity is estimated by assuming that the peak is 62–80% of installed capacity.
Opening date In the chart, the lower estimate uses the later opening date and the upper estimate the earlier date.

The map circles, zone totals and capacity bars show the upper estimate. The chart below the map shows the full range. The ranges reflect the reported figures and the assumptions described below. They are not statistical confidence intervals.

Capacity conversion

PUE (power usage effectiveness) is the electricity used by the whole site divided by the electricity used by its IT equipment. The tracker uses 1.2 unless a source reports a value for the site. In the EU reporting scheme, very large datacenters have an energy-weighted average PUE of 1.20. Country averages for 2025 were 1.23 in Sweden and 1.17 in Denmark. Finland reported 1.16 for 2024. Norway is outside the scheme. Epoch AI also uses 1.2 for hyperscale sites, while RISE (2023) reports 1.1–1.3 for efficient Swedish sites.

PUE is an annual average. Using it to convert power assumes a constant cooling share, so it can understate demand on the hottest days.

When IT capacity is missing, the project details estimate it by dividing site power by PUE. A PUE given by the source takes precedence over the default of 1.2. Grid capacity and MW with no stated measure are treated as site power. Peak demand is first converted to installed capacity. Each estimate keeps the building, phase or campus named in the source.

Annual electricity use

Annual electricity use (TWh) = site MW in use × load factor × 8,760 / 1,000,000

A load factor of 0.75 means that average demand over the year is 75% of the site's installed capacity. One load profile applies to the whole physical campus, including its buildings and customer installations.

The factors come from EPRI's measurements at US sites (2026): one large facility with a single physical user and several smaller facilities with multiple tenants. Large campuses with multiple tenants were not measured. The tracker uses the range between the two observations for these campuses. For comparison, NVE (2026) uses 0.70 of reserved grid capacity for Norwegian datacenters. The tracker factors have not been validated against Nordic operating data.

Campus size is assessed from its planned capacity at full build-out. Site power is divided by PUE to estimate IT power where necessary. The load-profile rule uses 50 MW IT to distinguish large from small campuses. The datacenter types below are classified separately. Load factors follow the campus rules in the table above.

Electricity use at full build-out, shown on the map and in the project list, assumes that all announced capacity is built and in use. Annual electricity use in the chart follows the opening dates and ramp-up described below. It is added to the 2025 consumption of existing sites, which is held constant. In its first operating year, a project receives its first-year share for the whole year, regardless of the opening month.

Datacenter types

The types describe how a site is used or what it is being built for. They can overlap. A hyperscale platform may rent space from a colocation operator, and any type of datacenter can run AI workloads.

Type Definition
Hyperscale Large datacenters used by cloud, internet or AI platforms, including campuses being built for these customers. The tracker uses project announcements and the operator's services to check the site's actual or intended customers. Planned projects can be classified as hyperscale before a customer is named or a contract is signed.
Colocation The operator provides space, power and cooling for customers' IT equipment. This includes racks, halls and entire buildings built for a customer.
Enterprise An organisation runs the facility for its own computing needs. This category also includes shared public research computing.
AI / HPC The site runs or is being built for artificial intelligence or high-performance computing. This describes what the computers do and can apply to any business model.

Hyperscale sites often exceed 50 MW IT, although smaller platform facilities are also included. Classification follows the site's actual or planned use. These campuses can serve a mix of customers. The category describes the campus as a whole. Contracts with hyperscalers may cover only part of its capacity.

The definitions are based on EPRI (2026, footnote 2), ENTSO-E (2026, section 1.2) and Regen / National Grid (2025). Their definitions differ in how they treat ownership and rented facilities. The tracker records overlapping types and notes whether the use is planned, contracted or already operating.

In the electricity view, the totals by bidding zone are also shown as a share of electricity consumption in 2025. The comparison uses eSett Open Data, summed over the year: 126.7 TWh in Sweden, 139.2 TWh in Norway, 82.9 TWh in Finland and 39.8 TWh in Denmark. These shares show the scale of the proposed additions compared with current consumption. Project calculations use the capacity figures and load factors described above.

Stages

Each capacity figure is assigned a stage from the decisions, construction activity and dates in its sources. The ticks show which scenario includes each stage.

Opening dates and ramp-up

Reported opening dates are used where available. If a source gives only a construction start, operation is assumed to begin years later. If neither date is available, the estimate depends on project status:

Gradual use of capacity
Without a reported year of full operation, each building or phase reaches full use over years, including its opening year. This applies even if the building itself opens all at once. A reported or observed year of full operation takes precedence.
Remaining campus capacity
Where a campus has a dated phase under construction or backed by an investment decision, the remaining undated capacity follows that phase's estimated opening date and the same ramp-up. Reported phase deliveries are used where available.
Grid connection
A reported connection year sets the earliest possible opening date. If the connection is only applied for or its status is unclear, the lower estimate in Confirmed and Dated plans counts no operating capacity.
After 2035
Capacity expected to come into use after 2035 falls outside the chart, but remains in the map's full announced size.

The lead times are modelling assumptions. IEA (2025) describes construction periods of two to three years. NVE (2026) finds about five years from a grid reservation to the first building in Norway, followed by three to five years to full operation.

The five-year ramp-up follows EPRI's calibration against US state-level data, including colocation. Its use for Nordic projects is an assumption. A source's full-capacity date may refer to completed buildings or a ready grid connection, with customers moving in later. Using that date for full operation can overstate electricity use in the early years.

Data coverage and country comparisons

Totals cover projects with a usable MW figure. Several projects have none, including some large developments. Differences between countries therefore reflect both their announced projects and the information available about them.

Norway's Statnett publishes project names and MW for reserved connections and its capacity queue. Sweden's Svenska kraftnät publishes aggregate queue figures. Finland's Fingrid treats connection applications as confidential. Planning and permit documents provide further site-level information, although they do not always disclose electrical capacity.

How the tracker is updated

Each country is reviewed using the same search instructions. Sources, earlier announcements and unresolved questions are kept with each project.

01Search for new capacity

An AI research agent searches company announcements, planning and permit records, grid information and local news. It works country by country, following written instructions and search terms in Swedish, Norwegian, Finnish, Danish and English.

A project is added when sources link a named datacenter company or customer to a specific site and to new capacity. I review and approve every new project before it is included.

02Check project identity, phases and dates

Each announcement is checked against the projects and earlier names already recorded. Addresses, land parcels, company relationships and previous announcements help distinguish a new site from an expansion or a change of name.

Capacity figures, dates and investment decisions are recorded for the building, phase or campus they describe. A date for one building is not applied to the entire campus. Overlapping announcements are counted once.

Historic names, superseded targets and earlier capacity claims are retained with their sources.

03Record what each MW figure measures

Every MW figure is recorded as IT power, site power, grid capacity or another stated measure. Its dates, project status and investment decision are kept with the part of the project they describe.

Source ranges and qualifications are retained. Conflicting figures and unresolved relationships are flagged for follow-up, with the questions linked to the relevant projects and sources. The calculations use the rules described above.

04Calculate and review the totals

The map, chart, table and downloadable data all come from the same calculation. Results are checked for overlapping capacity, missing information and conflicts between sources before the site is updated.

New projects are reviewed one by one before they are included in the totals.

05Identify actors and global headquarters

Project announcements and site records identify the developer, operator, named customer and any property owner or project partner. Their roles are recorded separately. A partnership does not create extra capacity.

Global headquarters are checked using company reports, official contact pages and announcements. Headquarters refers to the company group, so a local office or subsidiary may have a different address. Registration, headquarters and financial ownership are recorded separately.

Ownership notes describe the ownership reported in each source and the date of that report. The HQ filter groups projects by where their companies are headquartered. Financial ownership is recorded separately. Unknown headquarters stay unknown. A project can match a known partner's HQ even when another partner is unidentified. Expand a project to see the companies, roles, headquarters and sources.

HQ selection filters the map and its totals without splitting capacity between actors.
