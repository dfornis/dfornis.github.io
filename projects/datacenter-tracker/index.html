Nordic datacenter tracker

This page tracks publicly reported plans for new datacenter projects in Sweden, Norway and Finland. The dataset is being developed for an upcoming research article estimating the impact of datacenter load additions on electricity prices in Nordic bidding zones. The tracker compiles publicly reported project information from press releases, media reports, permitting documents and company material.

The Nordic countries are currently attracting substantial interest from datacenter developers due to cheap renewable electricity, climate conditions and land availability. I'm sharing the dataset here as a public resource and to open the project for comments and suggestions. The methodology is still under development. In particular, I am working on improving how heterogeneous reported MW figures should be interpreted, and how PUE and annual load factors should be assigned. Corrections, missing projects, better source material and methodological comments are very welcome, please send them to fornborg@kth.se.

Capacity and electricity use

Full build-out (MW) is the additional site capacity if all announced plans are completed, including cooling and other site electricity use. Reported site power and grid connection figures are used as stated. IT capacity is converted to site power using a PUE of 1.1–1.5, or a reported design PUE. If a source does not say what its MW figure measures, the figure is retained without PUE conversion.

Overlapping phases are counted once. Capacity already operating at the end of 2025 is subtracted, while later additions remain in the annual figures after completion. A project included in a larger campus is counted there and shows “In campus” in the table. Expand the row to see the original figures, dates and sources. Backup generators and power generation are excluded from datacenter load.

Electricity at full build-out (TWh/yr) is full build-out multiplied by the annual load factor and 8,760 hours. The load factors are 0.75 for hyperscale, 0.57 for colocation, 0.60 for colocation with AI, and 0.80 for AI/HPC. These assumptions draw on EPRI (2026), IEA (2025) and the documented model method. This calculation estimates annual electricity use at that level of capacity.

Opening dates and construction. The model uses a reported opening date where one is available. Otherwise it estimates when the datacenter could open from the construction date or its current stage of development. Dates and investment decisions apply to the building or phase they describe. The first building’s opening date does not tell us when the rest of the site will be ready.

A published timetable for individual phases takes precedence over general assumptions. Where the pace of expansion is unknown, capacity is normally added over five years from the first opening. A development completed in one phase is counted in its opening year. A reported completion target can shorten the assumed construction and expansion periods.

Larger sites built in phases. Stated can include a site without an overall timetable when one of its datacenter phases is under construction or has a confirmed investment decision, and a construction, opening or completion date is available for that phase. This can establish an estimated opening date for the site even when the first phase’s MW is unknown.

For these sites, the model assumes that capacity increases by an equal amount each year from first operation until 2035. It calculates an earlier and a later opening date to allow for uncertainty. Completion in 2035 is an assumption. Individual phases with known capacity keep their announced schedules. Low still requires a date and an investment decision for the capacity being counted.

Grid connections. In Low and Stated, a later grid connection date delays the assumed opening. If the connection is still only requested or its status is unclear, the lower estimate can be zero. The reported connection capacity does not cap the model’s estimate of datacenter capacity. High assumes all announced capacity is built by 2035, so it does not apply these grid restrictions and brings later completion dates forward to that year.

How the tracker is updated

Each country update follows the same sequence from initial lead to published scenario result. The steps below explain what information is collected, how separate projects and phases are identified, and how the source material becomes the figures shown in the table and map.

01Search for new capacity

A rules-based search routine works through each country using a documented codebook. It combines operator and customer names with place names, capacity terms, construction stages and grid terminology. The search covers company announcements, planning and permit records, grid information, local reporting and other public sources.

A lead moves forward when the sources connect a named datacenter operator or end customer to a specific location and a new capacity addition. The source trail and unresolved questions are retained for the next step.

02Reconcile identity, phases and dates

The lead is compared with existing projects, candidate records and historic names. Addresses, municipalities, land parcels, company relationships and earlier announcements help determine whether it is a new project, a new phase or another name for an existing campus.

Campus owners, operators, tenants and individual buildings are linked without merging their evidence. Each phase keeps the MW figure, date and investment decision stated for that phase. This prevents double counting and keeps a building’s dates separate from those of the wider campus.

A target omitted or replaced by a later source for the same or comparable scope is treated as undated until newly dated evidence appears. The original target is retained in the source detail and marked as superseded.

03Record what each source actually measures

Every capacity claim is recorded with its unit, scope and qualifier. IT equipment load, total site electricity use and grid connection capacity remain distinct. The record also keeps the source's range, project status, timing and investment decision, together with the part of the campus or phase to which they apply.

Conflicting figures are represented as a range when the evidence supports one. Missing figures remain unknown. A statement such as “at least X MW” is calculated using X in full build-out and eligible scenarios, with the normal conversion for its measure. Its qualifier is preserved beside the original source figure. If an included claim exceeds its parent campus after conversion, both sources go to follow-up. The largest applicable scope continues to determine the group’s capacity while the discrepancy is unresolved.

04Calculate and review the scenarios

The scenario definitions apply to the map, chart and table. Results are checked for overlapping capacity, missing information and conflicts between sources before the site is updated.

New records enter as candidates. Their identity, evidence and scenario effect are reviewed before inclusion in the totals. One approved calculation feeds the data export, annual chart, summary, table and map.

05Identify actors and global headquarters

Project announcements and site-specific sources identify the developer, operator, named customer and, where relevant, property owner or project partner. Each actor is linked to the whole project, including its phases. Partnerships keep separate actors and roles.

Global headquarters are checked against company reports, official contact pages and company announcements, with other reliable sources used when needed. A local subsidiary's registered address or a regional office does not establish the group's global HQ. Registration country, headquarters, fund manager and financial owners are recorded separately. The business type describes what the actor does.

Ownership notes retain the source and date. They describe the documented ownership without assigning an owner nationality or tracing every investor. Unknown identities and HQ countries remain unknown and do not match any HQ group. A project can still match a known partner's HQ while another actor is unresolved. Expand the project for actors, roles, HQ, sources and the review date.

The reviews cover 31 Swedish, 46 Finnish and 32 Norwegian projects as of 17 October 2026. Every relevant actor has a known HQ country for 30 Swedish, 42 Finnish and 30 Norwegian projects. The remaining cases include an anonymous operator, a company with two headquarters and actors that do not publish one global HQ. HQ selection filters the map and its totals without allocating capacity between actors.
